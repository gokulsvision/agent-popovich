# Press lines

Where the team starts applying pressure. Decide it before the match. Two broad families: high, in their third, and half-court everywhere else. People disagree on the exact boundary. Say which you mean.

**Format:** futsal. On 11v11 the rec default is: one trigger, otherwise drop. A high press that needs four coordinated runners will not survive Tuesday with eight in attendance.

## High press

Win it near their goal and you can shoot immediately. Psychological pressure.

Cost: huge space behind. Distances between defenders grow, cover arrives late. After you win it, there is little space to attack into, so the finish has to be quick.

The Y-shaped (epsilon) zone is the common picture. Front three plus a last man.

Roles:

- **1st defender:** press the ball carrier
- **2nd defender:** screen the skip pass (ala to ala)
- **3rd defender:** float and cover, and talk

When the 1st is actually pressing, the opponent cannot easily play in behind or between the lines. Then the 2nd stays and keeps screening the horizontal. The 3rd should call **stay** or **hold**.

If the 1st is **not** close enough, the golden rule is the 2nd protects the central player so the layers survive. If the 3rd steps up instead, the structure flattens and two-player combinations go through.

If the 1st **is** pressing and cutting the central lane, the 2nd can ignore the central player, screen the ala-to-ala, and hunt the ball.

Typical 3rd-defender calls: **stay**, **go with him**.

On a goal clearance, do not jump straight into the Y if both alas push up and nobody knows who follows whom. Many teams start man-to-man and only build the lines after the ball goes wide.

Cue: **if you are not winning it in two seconds, you are not pressing. Drop.**

## Half-court

Defenders are close together. Cover is easier. You reduce the space they can use. There is room to counter.

Cost: the goal is close, so they have shooting options. After you win it you are a long way from their goal.

The rotating diamond is the orthodox half-court shape.

Cue: **compact. Do not get pulled into their half without a trigger.**

## Rec translation

One press trigger: a bad touch, a pass we can reach, a keeper with no outlet. Otherwise drop as a block. Chasing as five individuals is how rec teams get run through.

On 11v11, a high press is a 40-yard recovery if it fails. Rec recovery will not support that as a default.

## Do not install

A 40-minute high press. Y-shape teaching on a team that does not yet drop as a block. Pressing with no trigger.
