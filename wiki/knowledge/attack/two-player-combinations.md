# Two-player combinations

The smallest attacking unit. Everything larger is these, repeated.

**Format:** futsal first. Wall pass and third-man travel to 11v11. Parallel and diagonal are court geometry. Do not quote them as 40-yard runs.

## Wall pass (give-and-go)

Pass, sprint into the space you just created, receive it back. The original futsal combination. Works because the first defender turns toward the ball and the runner is already gone.

Trigger: a defender is committed to you.
Failure: the passer stands. The return has nobody to hit.
Cue: **pass and go. Standing is the error.**

## Parallel

A run in behind, parallel to a teammate, into the vacated channel. In quatro this is often a central parallel between the two deep players, or a double run that creates two forward lanes at once.

Trigger: the next line is empty. Their back line is dropping or turned.
Failure: the run is made when numbers are even and a loss is a 3v1. Rest defence first.
Cue: **if the lane is empty, occupy it. If it is not, hold.**

On 11v11 this is taking space. Same idea, different distance.

## Diagonal

A run that cuts across lanes, not along them. The point is to drag a marker out of their lane so a teammate can fill the one they left.

Trigger: a defender is square, watching the ball.
Failure: two players end up in the destination lane.
Cue: **cut across. Someone fill what you left.**

## Backdoor (porta)

A run behind a defender who has just switched marks or turned toward the ball. Used specifically against teams that exchange marks on a 3 on line. The third-man through-ball is dead against a clean switch. The backdoor is the answer: attack the space the switch just vacated.

Trigger: they switched. The original marker left a hole.
Failure: running backdoor into a defender who did not switch. You run into cover.
Cue: **they switched. Go where they left.**

## Skip pass

A pass that misses the nearest option and finds the far one. Used off 3 on line to the far ala. If the middle defender jumps to intercept, the short wide pocket (ala corta) opens.

Trigger: the near option is marked, the far one is free.
Failure: a slow skip across your own half. Interception is a break.
Cue: **miss the first man if the far one is on. Not in our own D.**

## Block / curtain (pick)

An attacker sets a body on a marker so a teammate can receive free. At the top level this is how man-to-man is beaten (pick and roll). The defence's answer is to switch marks, not to fight through.

Trigger: man-to-man, a teammate needs a yard.
Failure: a reckless screen that is a foul, or a block that nobody uses.
Cue: **make the marker decide. Then use the free man.**

Rec translation: do not teach picks as a system. A body in the way on a kick-in is enough.

## Third-man

Two players combine. A third arrives late into the space they opened. Keeps the attack flowing when the first return is marked.

Trigger: the wall pass is covered.
Failure: all three occupy the same pocket.
Cue: **two play, the third arrives.**

## Rec translation

Teach wall pass and "run the empty lane." Name nothing else until those two show on tape. Third-man is the next one. Parallel, diagonal, backdoor, skip, block wait.

## Do not install

A combination catalogue as a session plan. Court names on an 11v11 pitch. Picks as a primary attacking method for a rec team that still stands after passing.
