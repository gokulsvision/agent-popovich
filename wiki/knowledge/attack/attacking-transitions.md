# Attacking transitions

The five seconds after a win. The other five seconds are defensive transition. Same window, opposite jobs.

Elite futsal is often decided here, not in a patient 4-0. Match analysis of elite goals found a large share coming from defence-to-offence sequences and from restarts created by the laws, not only from organised positional attack. See [[analysis-metrics]].

**Format:** principle. Distances live on the match page (`11v11` or `futsal`).

## After a win

First thought forward. If the next pocket is free, occupy it. Width first, then height. The passer does not stand.

Trigger: we win it, their back line is dropping or turned, numbers are with us.
Failure: we take space that is occupied, or we all run and leave nobody behind the ball. A loss then is a 3v1 the other way.
Cue: **win it. First thought forward. Count who is behind.**

On futsal the counter is two or three passes. On 11v11 it can be a 40-yard race. Do not quote court geometry on a pitch.

## After a loss

Rest defence first, then recover. Rec default after an attacking corner: three behind the ball. Two is a gamble. One is a concession.

Trigger: we lose it.
Failure: chasing as five individuals. That is how rec teams get run through.
Cue: **ball lost. If we cannot win it in two seconds, drop.**

The keeper's job in the same window is come or stay. See [[come-off-the-line]].

## Short counter from a high press

If you win it near their goal, shoot. That is the whole point of a high press. The cost is the space you gave behind you if you do not win it.

Cue: **win it high, finish it. Do not take an extra pass you do not need.**

## Rec translation

One picture for the team: the moment the ball changes feet, the picture changes. First body movement in that window is the note. Train that, not two different sports.

## Do not install

A counter-pressing syllabus. A rule that we always go, or always drop. The tape decides. Last week's theme stays until it lands.
