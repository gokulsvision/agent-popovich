# Match demands

What the game asks of a body. Trainer reads this before inventing gym work. Film still decides whether the problem is physical or knowledge.

Not medical advice. Load is not diagnosis.

## Intermittent, not steady

Futsal is repeated high-intensity efforts and changes of direction, not a long run. Work-to-rest is stop-start. Players who test better on intermittent fitness (for example the 30-15 IFT) sustain more high-intensity accelerations across congested fixtures.

In a multi-day tournament, raw sprinting distance and sprint duration dropped about 27–29% by matches 4–6 in one study, while passing success rose. Tired legs, simpler decisions.

11v11 rec is also stop-start, but the distances are longer and the rest between sprints is less clean. Do not copy a futsal conditioning block onto a Saturday 11v11 week without looking at [[PROTOCOL]].

## What to train, in order

1. Repeated-sprint ability and first-step acceleration
2. Deceleration and change of direction (this is where knees and ankles go)
3. Balance and single-leg stability
4. Aerobic base last, and never as the week's theme if the tape showed a positioning problem

Agility ladders and cone drills are tools. They are not the session. The session is the week's one thing, at the volume recovery allows.

## Keeper-specific

Lateral set, first-step to come or stay, reaction at close range. Extra diving only on green, and only if the tape showed a dive problem. Rec keepers over-dive. Default is set. See [[FROM-GAMEPLAY]] and [[keeper-physical]].

## Rec translation

Two team sessions and one GK session around a match. One theme. Yellow recovery: technical and decisions, no extra plyo. Red: walkthrough, film, mobility.

## Do not install

A strength-and-conditioning syllabus. Long slow runs as futsal prep. Extra diving on a yellow day.
