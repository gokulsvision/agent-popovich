# Analysis metrics

What a video room actually tags, and where goals come from. Use this to name a pattern. Do not turn a rec match into a spreadsheet.

Detectors in this repo find scene cuts and audio peaks. They cannot see a turnover. You still read the frames.

## Where elite futsal goals come from

From notational analysis of elite matches (Sarmento et al., *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 2016, PMID 26183125):

- **42%** positional attack
- **27%** set pieces
- Transitions (defence to offence) and restarts created by the laws were the most common sequence types that produced goals
- **73%** of goals from central offensive zones, not wide
- The foot was the main scoring surface

So: set plays are not a footnote. Central finishes beat pretty wing play. The five seconds after a win are not optional.

From possession analysis of close Spanish League playoff games (Sarmento et al., 2015, PMID 26280547):

- Individual defence, set play, and **0–3 passes** related to unsuccessful possessions
- Higher effectiveness: stopped balls or set plays that ended in specific attacking zones and used **more than 4 passes**
- Absence of defensive pressure increased both the chance of losing it and the chance of being effective if you did not (you can be free and still waste it)

Patience against a set defence is not philosophy. It is the data.

5v4 with a fly keeper changes scoring patterns by game state (Méndez et al., 2019, PMID 31304885). Do not read a power-play goal as a positional-attack lesson.

## What to tag on our tape

Required already: `goal_for`, `goal_against`, `turnover_lost`, `turnover_won`.

Useful extra, when you can see it:

- sequence type: positional / transition / set piece / restart
- zone of the shot: central vs wide
- pass count in the possession, roughly (0–3 vs 4+)
- unit: O / D / GK
- knowledge vs physical

Do not tag what the tape cannot support. Shirt colours and formation need to be on the match stub before you name a person.

## Rec translation

After a match, three questions:

1. Did the goals come from a set picture, a break, or a restart.
2. Did we shoot from the middle or from nowhere.
3. Did we move it enough against a set defence, or did we give it away on pass two.

That is the video room. Then one thing to train.
