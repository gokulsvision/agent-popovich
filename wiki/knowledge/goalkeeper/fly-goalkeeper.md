# Fly goalkeeper

The keeper joins the attack. Brazilians: goleiro-linha. Often called a power play. Most often used when you are losing and want a fifth attacker.

This is a footnote on [[offense]] and [[goalie]], not a fourth unit.

**Format:** futsal. Rec 11v11 almost never does this. Do not install it on a pitch.

## The laws that make it possible

- In the **opposition half** the keeper may play as long as they like with their feet.
- In **their own half** they still have four seconds, and cannot receive it back until an opponent touches it.
- Any substitute may change with the keeper without stopping play, but must wear a keeper jersey (a bib is fine at amateur level). Teams sometimes send on a specialist outfield player in a keeper shirt.

## When teams use it

Chasing the game. Sometimes as a designed 5v4 in a set attack, not only as panic. Sometimes to play out of a press.

## How it actually works

You outnumber their four. The extra body is the free man. Rotations that already create a free man now create two. Shooting trigger is the same as positional attack: a lane, a bounce, a kick-in.

The risk is total. A loss is an empty net. Rest defence is the keeper's old job, and they are not there.

## Defending it

Do not all chase the extra player. Protect the goal. One stays. Foul only if you are past the accumulated-foul limit with your eyes open: a 10m kick against an empty net is also a goal.

Cue: **they have five. We do not all go. One stays, the rest compact.**

## Rec translation

Do not use this until the last minutes of a game you are losing, and only if the keeper can play. A rec keeper charging out because they saw it on YouTube is how you lose 6-3 instead of 4-3.

Until this team actually pulls the keeper, it stays a footnote.

## Do not install

A power-play syllabus. Flying keeper as a default in a 3-1. An 11v11 sweeper-keeper copied from this page.
